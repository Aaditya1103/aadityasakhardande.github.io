June 2025 - August 2025

Product Engineer (Leyline Technologies)


During my time as a Product Engineer at Leyline Technologies in Spokane, WA, I worked remotely to support AI model research and workflow design for the company's robotics-adjacent product line. The role combined applied machine learning work with product engineering, under the mentorship of the team's senior engineers, and required adapting research-stage models into something closer to a usable, well-documented product feature.

Role Overview

Leyline Technologies builds products where AI models sit close to real-time robotics systems, meaning a model isn't just judged on offline accuracy, it has to behave predictably inside a live product pipeline. My work sat at that intersection: researching and documenting model designs, iterating on data-driven prototypes, and working with other functions to make sure the resulting features fit cleanly into the broader product.

AI Model Design and Documentation

I was tasked with designing and documenting three AI models aimed at optimizing product features in alignment with the company's real-time robotics systems. This meant more than writing training scripts: each model needed a clear specification of its inputs, outputs, and constraints so that other engineers could integrate it without needing to reverse-engineer my assumptions.
Because these models fed into systems operating in real time, I paid close attention to how a model's behavior at the edges (uncommon inputs, timing constraints, failure modes) would surface downstream, and documented those considerations alongside the models themselves rather than leaving them as tribal knowledge.

Data-Driven Prototype Iteration

Alongside model design, I iterated on two data-driven prototypes, using Python and Leyline's internal tooling to refine both model accuracy and the clarity of the surrounding data pipeline. Over the course of the internship, this iteration improved model accuracy and pipeline clarity by 20%.
Much of this improvement came from tightening the feedback loop between data preparation and model evaluation: cleaning up how data moved through the pipeline made it easier to isolate whether a change in performance came from the model itself or from an upstream data issue, which in turn made each iteration faster and more informative than the last.

Cross-Functional Collaboration and Modular Design

I worked with cross-functional teams to translate product requirements into modular designs suited for robot-adjacent use cases, where a feature often needs to work correctly on its own while also composing cleanly with other parts of the system. This required regularly reconciling what a feature needed to do from a product standpoint with what was practical to build and maintain from an engineering standpoint.
Keeping designs modular made it possible for different pieces of the system to evolve independently, which mattered in a fast-moving, remote-first environment where research findings and product requirements were both still being refined in parallel.

Impact and Applications

The work from this internship fed directly into Leyline's product roadmap for AI-enabled, robotics-adjacent features. In particular, it contributed to:

  • Faster iteration cycles for the product team, with clearer documentation reducing the ramp-up time needed to build on top of a given model.
  • More reliable real-time behavior, since model constraints and edge cases were identified and documented early rather than discovered in production.
  • Cleaner integration between AI components and the rest of the product, thanks to the emphasis on modular design across teams.

This experience strengthened my ability to work across the boundary between AI research and product engineering, and reinforced how much of building reliable, real-time AI systems comes down to disciplined documentation and design, not just model performance in isolation.
